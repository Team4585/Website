You don’t need to know how to build a robot when you show up. Everyone eventually has to explain their work to someone else.

Why the team exists

Build a permanent and visible presence in our community.

Students practice engineering, programming, business, and leadership on a robot that has to be ready by a fixed date, finished or not.

Our motto is “Non satis prope est,” close isn’t good enough. A mechanism that almost works gets rebuilt. Wiring that almost works gets redone. The Impact presentation gets another rewrite.

How the work is divided

Three divisions, each run by students

01

Engineering

Sketch ideas, test cardboard and scrap-metal prototypes, build the robot in Onshape, machine parts in our shop, wire electronics, and write the code. We certify as many students on the shop machines as we can.

02

Operations

Run outreach and summer camps, write grants and award submissions, raise money, and handle media.

03

Performance

Study the game, decide which parts of it are worth chasing, pick the drive team, and scout other robots.

After school at Hillcrest

Every meeting starts with the whole team in one room.

We meet after school Monday through Thursday until 6 p.m. After the whole-team start, everyone splits into subteams. Attendance is flexible. Our mentors are Clief Castleton, Craig Conder, and Kevin Wood. Email lead mentor Clief Castleton before your first visit so we know to expect you.

For families

What students need

  • Willingness to say yes when the team asks for help (that’s what the cheer below means)
  • A FIRST account and current consent forms before competition
  • Communication with the mentor about schedules and travel
  • No membership fees and no team travel fees.

Registration steps can change. The mentor will send families the current forms and deadlines.

Team cheer

“Huskies jump!” “How high?”

It’s our cheer at competitions. It also means that when someone on the team is asked to do something, the answer is yes.
