Serritor went to Idaho and Utah

We built our 2026 robot around WCP swerve modules, an over-the-bumper intake, a triple-ball shooter, and an extendable hopper.

Serritor started the same way most of our robots do: a game strategy, a pile of cardboard, and several ideas that worked better in CAD than they did on the floor. Students designed the mechanisms in Onshape, machined parts, wired the electronics, and wrote the code while the robot changed around them.

The final robot used WCP Swerve XS modules, an over-the-bumper intake, a triple-ball shooter, and an extendable hopper. We brought Serritor to the Idaho Regional and Utah Regional. Those events gave the drive team time on the field and gave everyone else a long list of repairs and improvements to make between matches.

That process matters as much as the finished machine. A student who spent the fall learning one CAD tool may end the season rebuilding an assembly in the pits with a match coming up. Serritor gave this year’s larger team plenty of real problems to solve together.
